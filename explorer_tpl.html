mock Experimental food nutrition explorer, not part of the main site. Six ways to look at the same public USDA data. ← Back to Food Log

About the data: every value is per 100 g of food from USDA FoodData Central (fdc.nal.usda.gov), accessed @@ACC@@. “no data” means USDA reports no value for that nutrient, which is not the same as zero. Foods marked ≈ stand-in use a USDA entry that approximates the real food (different brand, type or preparation), so treat them as rough. Bars are % of a daily reference for an adult man 51+ (see the source note at the bottom). Raw foods and cooked foods differ a lot per gram, so check the entry name.
A ProfileB LeaderboardC HeatmapD Top 5E CompareF Live lookup

A) Food profile card

Every nutrient USDA reports for one food, grouped, with bars for % of daily reference.

B) Nutrient leaderboard

Pick a nutrient and see every food ranked from highest to lowest. Foods with no data are listed at the end.

    C) Sortable heatmap

    Per 100 g. Darker cell = bigger % of the daily reference. Click or press a column header to sort (again to reverse). Sodium is shaded amber because it is a limit, not a goal.

    Grey “no data” cells are not zeros.

    D) Top 5 per nutrient

    What’s high in… Per 100 g of food as listed (so dried or fatty foods such as seeds rank high; check typical portion sizes before drawing conclusions).

    E) Nutrient fingerprint and compare

    12 nutrients as % of the daily reference per 100 g, capped at 100% at the outer ring. Pick two foods to overlay them. A missing value is drawn at the center and marked “?”.

    F) Live USDA lookup

    Search any food straight from USDA FoodData Central (SR Legacy and Foundation entries) and see the same profile card. This calls the public USDA API from your browser with the shared DEMO_KEY, which is rate limited (about 30 requests an hour per network), so results are cached for this tab. If it fails, the list above already includes pre-fetched sweet potato, kale, bok choy, collards and broccoli.

      Daily reference values used (adult man 51+): protein 56 g, fiber 30 g, calcium 1,000 mg, magnesium 420 mg, potassium 3,400 mg, sodium 2,300 mg limit, phosphorus 700 mg, iron 8 mg, zinc 11 mg, copper 0.9 mg, manganese 2.3 mg, selenium 55 µg, vitamin A 900 µg RAE, C 90 mg, D 15 µg, E 15 mg, K 120 µg, B1 1.2 mg, B2 1.3 mg, B3 16 mg, B5 5 mg, B6 1.7 mg, folate 400 µg DFE, B12 2.4 µg, choline 550 mg: DRIs from the National Academies (NAM), as summarized in the NIH Office of Dietary Supplements fact sheets. Energy 2,000 kcal, total fat 78 g, saturated fat 20 g (limit), carbohydrate 275 g, added sugars 50 g (limit), cholesterol 300 mg (limit): FDA Daily Values. No reference is shown for trans, mono- and polyunsaturated fat, total sugars, water, caffeine or alcohol. Educational only, not medical advice.
