Review & Summary

Prepared Oct 11, 2026 for a 54-year-old man. Educational only, not medical advice; review with your doctor or pharmacist. Figures marked "est." are my estimates; label values come from the product pages/labels listed in the table above or that I looked up.

Bottom line

  • Overall: a reasonable, mostly safe regimen with no dangerous overlaps found. Nothing is near a toxic level except vitamin D3 at 5,000 IU, which is above the 4,000 IU/day upper limit (UL) used by the Institute of Medicine/NIH. Many clinicians accept this dose, but it should be confirmed with a 25-OH vitamin D blood test.
  • Calcium: from your Oct 10 food log I estimate roughly 900–1,200 mg/day, close to the 1,000 mg RDA, almost all from milk and latte. No calcium pill is needed if that day is typical. Do not add one without a reason, especially with D3 at 5,000 IU.
  • Liver label discrepancy resolved: the figures you pasted (B12 ~1.4 mcg, vitamin A ~2,830 mcg, iron ~3 mg) do not match Ancestral Supplements' own label for 6 capsules (3,000 mg): vitamin A 1,391 mcg RAE (1,385 mcg retinol), B12 13.6 mcg, choline 120 mg, iron ~2 mg. Use the manufacturer's numbers. Check your bottle.
  • Fix the timing: take zinc away from the milk, magnesium and any iron-containing item (liver) by about 2 hours if possible.
  • Clarify doses: fish oil, magnesium, and Garden of Life vitamin C are labeled per 2 units; you take 1 of some (see below), so actual intake may be half of the label.

Brands & manufacturers

  • Pure Encapsulations (iodine): Strong. Its Sudbury, MA facilities are NSF-listed for cGMP (NSF/ANSI 455-2), and it states each batch is tested for identity, potency, heavy metals, and microbes. NSF GMP covers the facility, not each product. Owned by Nestlé Health Science. No major recalls found.
  • Nordic Naturals (fish oil): Strong reputation. Publishes a lot-number COA tool and tests against European Pharmacopoeia, GOED, and Prop 65 limits. I could not confirm an IFOS or USP certification listing, so it is brand-reported testing. Keep fish oil in a cool place; smell for rancidity.
  • Nature Made (magnesium): Large US maker; many of its products carry the USP Verified mark. I could not confirm that this specific magnesium bisglycinate product is USP Verified, so look for the USP seal on your bottle.
  • Jarrow Formulas (glucosamine/chondroitin): Verified: two voluntary recalls on record, neither for this product: Liquid L-Carnitine lot JLC17131 (Jan 2018, impurities; made by an outside contract manufacturer) and a Class II mislabeling recall of N-A-C (Oct 2020, terminated 2021). (Jarrow press release; FDA recall D-0302-2021 via NDCList; accessed 2026-10-14). Unverified: NSF cGMP certification. The NSF cGMP claim I found is on the site of Jarrow Industries, a contract manufacturer whose link to Jarrow Formulas I couldn't confirm (source, accessed 2026-10-14). Unverified: whether the company has any FDA warning letters; my search found none.
  • Doctor's Best (ubiquinol): Verified: the product uses Kaneka ubiquinol, as the product name shows. Kaneka advises looking for its Kaneka Quality Seal on the package to confirm authenticity (Kaneka, Jul 2026, accessed 2026-10-14). Independent reviewers found no recalls in FDA's enforcement database, no FDA warning letters, no USP/NSF certification and no public batch COAs. ConsumerLab reportedly approved 14 of 15 Doctor's Best products it tested (UsefulVitamins; Suplmnt; accessed 2026-10-14). Unverified: the brand's own "cGMP" and "3rd Party tested" badges, which name no auditor or lab, and ConsumerLab results, which I didn't see first-hand because they are paywalled.
  • Garden of Life (vitamin C): Mainstream brand (Nestlé Health Science), whole-food/Non-GMO positioning, with raw blends. Whole-food vitamin C blends supply less measurable vitamin C per capsule than ascorbic acid, which is fine at this dose.
  • Superior Source (B12, D3+K2, zinc): Verified: made by Continental Vitamin Company, Vernon, CA, whose facility is listed by NSF as certified to NSF/ANSI 455-2 GMP for dietary supplements (powders and tablets) (NSF listing; Superior Source; accessed 2026-10-14). This certifies the facility's manufacturing practices, not each product's contents. The tablets contain lactose (per label). Unverified: any product-level third-party certification (USP/NSF/ConsumerLab), and the recall and warning-letter history (my searches found none, but I didn't do a full database check).
  • Ancestral Supplements (liver): Brand claims New Zealand/Australia grass-fed freeze-dried liver and "third-party tested for purity." Independent reviewers note no public batch COA and no USP/NSF seal; heavy-metal results are reportedly available on request. Liver concentrates contaminants, so email the company and ask for the current lot's heavy-metal COA (lead, cadmium, arsenic, mercury). No recalls found.
  • Forest Leaf (collagen): Brand states "GMP compliant, Made in USA" but shows no public heavy-metal results. The product is bovine plus chicken collagen. Independent 2020 testing by Clean Label Project of 28–30 top collagen brands found arsenic in 64% and lead in 37% (Forest Leaf's own result not shown). ConsumerLab also reported arsenic and/or lead in three collagen brands in 2025. At every-other-day use the risk is small but unverified; ask for a COA or consider a collagen with published testing.

Interactions & timing

  • Zinc and calcium/magnesium/iron compete for absorption. Your evening zinc lozenge taken with milk and magnesium is the least efficient pairing. Better: morning zinc on its own, and the second one mid-afternoon or with a non-dairy snack, not with milk.
  • Calcium and magnesium at night with milk is fine overall (and magnesium may help stool), but calcium can slightly reduce absorption of zinc and iron from liver. Take liver at lunch or dinner, away from milk and zinc.
  • Vitamin D3 + K2: sensible pairing. Fat-containing meals improve D absorption; take with lunch/dinner rather than a lozenge on an empty stomach.
  • Fish oil: mild blood-thinning effect at high doses (not at your dose). Tell your doctor before surgery. It can cause reflux or loose stool in some people.
  • Iodine and thyroid: your iodine plus dairy iodine is substantially above the 150 mcg RDA. Still under UL, but excess iodine can aggravate thyroid problems in susceptible people. TSH test advised.
  • Vitamin C and iron: vitamin C raises non-heme iron absorption; combined with liver iron, check ferritin if you ever get high results.
  • Digestion/stool: Magnesium glycinate (mild softening) helps. Vitamin C in higher doses can loosen stool. Fish oil can loosen slightly. Calcium-rich milk and low-fiber meals (sausage, pork, croissant) work the other way. CoQ10, B12, and collagen are neutral. The enzyme/probiotic blend may help a little but L. plantarum/bulgaricus effects are modest.
  • Lactose: Superior Source tablets contain lactose (tiny amounts, usually tolerated). If you feel bloating or gas from milk twice daily, try lactose-free milk (same calcium) or a lactase tablet.

Gaps, redundancies, and clarifications

  • Redundant or low value: B12 at 1,000 mcg is far above needs (but low-risk); vitamin C appears in three products; zinc and magnesium overlap is minor. CoQ10 exists in liver in trace amounts only.
  • Possible gaps: fiber (most important for stool softness: aim ~30 g/day; add chia, oats, beans, prunes, kiwi); omega-3 is on the low side at 1 gel; potassium (low intake of fruit/veg may matter); vitamin E and magnesium from food are low; iron is probably adequate via liver, but check ferritin. Some meals are low-fiber and low-fruit.
  • Dose ambiguity to resolve: fish oil (1 vs 2 gels), magnesium (label serving 2 caps = 200 mg; is it 1 or 2?), vitamin C (1 vs 2 caps; also the label you gave was cut off), glucosamine every other day vs daily.
  • Ask your doctor for bloodwork: 25-OH vitamin D, serum calcium, B12 (+ methylmalonic acid if borderline), ferritin/iron panel, zinc and copper, TSH, magnesium (RBC if available), CBC, kidney function, lipid panel (relevant to fish oil/CoQ10), and vitamin A (retinol) if you continue liver long term.
  • Tell your doctor/pharmacist about all supplements if you take any prescription (especially blood thinners, thyroid drugs, statins, blood pressure meds, antibiotics).
